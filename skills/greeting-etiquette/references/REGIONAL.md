# Regional greeting conventions

A short reference. These are broad norms, not rules — individuals and contexts vary.

## Japan

- A bow is the customary greeting; depth and duration signal respect. A slight
  bow suits casual settings, a deeper bow formal ones.
- Family name comes first, followed by the honorific **-san** (neutral and
  widely safe): *Tanaka-san*. Use **-sama** for notably higher deference.
- Avoid first names and physical contact until invited. Do not initiate a
  handshake unless the other person offers one.

## France

- A handshake is standard for formal and professional first meetings.
- *La bise* (a light cheek kiss, count varies by region) is common among
  acquaintances and in social settings — follow the other person's lead.
- Use **Monsieur** / **Madame** with the family name until invited to use a
  first name.

## United States

- A handshake and a first name are the common default across most settings.
- Register is generally casual; enthusiasm is well received in social contexts.
- Titles (Dr., Professor) matter in academic and clinical settings.

## India

- **Namaste** (palms together, a slight bow) is a widely respectful greeting.
- Honorifics such as **-ji** appended to a name show respect: *Sharma-ji*.
- Defer to elders first in a group.
